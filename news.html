HOW EARTHWORM BIO-CONVERSION IS REVIVING DEGRADED FARMLAND

By Mayur Sahebrao Phuse (B.Sc. Agriculture) • Farm Dispatch

Healthy soil is not merely a planting medium; it is a living ecosystem populated by billions of micro-organisms, beneficial fungi, and natural bio-converters. Over the past several decades, the over-reliance on synthetic fertilizers has accelerated the depletion of organic carbon across our farmlands, leaving soil compact, thirsty, and vulnerable to pest infestation.

At Siddhi Organics in Wavadade, Jalgaon, we harness nature's original soil engineers—Eisenia Fetida earthworms. Through controlled, low-heat decomposition of agricultural biomass and cow dung, earthworms produce premium humus rich in bio-available nitrates, phosphates, and active microbes. Farmers incorporating these producer-direct organic inputs observe immediate improvements in root aeration, water retention, and seasonal yield resilience.
